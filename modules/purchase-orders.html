Purchase Orders

A purchase order (PO) is how you buy stock from a vendor. It records what you've agreed to buy, follows it from the moment you commit to the vendor through every delivery that arrives, and — when you want it to — feeds what you receive into inventory. A single PO buys either raw materials or finished products, never both, and that one choice decides which inventory it touches.

What it does​

A purchase order does two jobs. You can lean on either, or both.

Track your vendor orders​

At its core a PO is a record: which vendor, what you ordered, at what price, when you placed it, and what has actually shown up so far. That record stands on its own — for a team that just wants its buying organised, a PO is a clean log of commitments and deliveries, with editable dates and costs, and no inventory needed.

Feed inventory​

When you do run inventory, the PO keeps your stock honest. Placing the order books the quantity as on order; receiving it moves that quantity to on hand. Each PO targets one inventory — materials for raw inputs, finished_goods for resale products — set by its targetType, so the nature of the operation decides which ledger is fed.

How it works​

The lifecycle​

A PO moves through a small set of statuses. The first three — Planning, Placed, In Transit — are yours to set by hand, and you can move between them in any direction to correct a mistake. From Placed or In Transit onward, receipts take over: recording a delivery is what carries the PO to Partial (some arrived) or Received (everything arrived). A received order can be marked Paid, and Cancelled is an exit available any time before goods start arriving.

Setting the status by hand​

  • Planning — you're still preparing the order. Nothing is committed and inventory is untouched.
  • Placed — you've committed to the vendor. The ordered quantity now shows as on order.
  • In Transit — the goods are on their way. (Informational today — it behaves like Placed.)

You can move among these three freely, forward or back. Stepped too far? Move back and the system undoes the effect cleanly. What you can't do is hand-move a PO down out of Partial or Received — those statuses belong to receipts.

Receiving​

Each delivery is a receipt with its own date and lines, and recording one moves the status for you:

  • a receipt that doesn't complete the order → Partial
  • the receipt that completes it → Received
  • delete receipts and the status walks back on its own — to Partial, or, once none remain, back to where the order was before anything arrived.

A receipt line's quantity can differ from what was ordered, which is how partial and over-deliveries are recorded.

Paying and cancelling​

  • A fully received PO can be marked Paid — and reverted to Received if that was a slip.
  • Cancelled stops an order before goods arrive. You can't cancel one that already has receipts (delete those first), and a cancelled PO can be reactivated — it returns to wherever it last was.

Dates​

Every PO carries dates, designed to stay truthful with the least fuss:

  • Order date is yours — it defaults to today when you create the PO and you can change it whenever.
  • Status dates (placed, in transit, …) stamp themselves as the PO reaches each status, but stay editable afterwards so you can correct them.
  • Partial and Received dates come straight from the receipts — the first receipt's date and the completing receipt's date — so they always match reality. Change a receipt's date and they follow.

When a date changes, the matching inventory movement re-dates with it, so the stock history always reads in true business-date order.

Inventory impact​

What you doEffect on the ledger
Place the order (leave Planning)books the ordered quantity as on order
Record a receiptmoves the received quantity to on hand
Cancelreverses the on-order quantity back to zero

It feeds the materials or finished-goods ledger per targetType, and every entry carries the PO number as its reference.

One simple rule keeps it tidy: anything you undo is erased, not stamped with a counter-entry. Move a PO back out of Placed and its on-order booking is simply removed; delete a receipt and its on-hand movement is removed. The only lasting reversal is a cancellation — and even that disappears if you reactivate the order. The ledger always reflects only what is true right now.

Open questions​

A few things we've deliberately parked — written down so they aren't lost.

  1. What really separates Placed from In Transit? Today both book the same on-order quantity and both accept receipts, so In Transit is purely informational. It stays a cosmetic step until it earns a real meaning.
  2. The allowed shortcuts are a symptom of that. Jumping Planning → In Transit (skipping Placed), or receiving straight from Placed (skipping In Transit), exist only because those two statuses aren't yet distinct. They'll be revisited together.
  3. Payment might deserve its own dimension. Paid is currently a single flag reached only from a fully received order. In reality an order can be paid before it's fully received, so payment may belong on its own axis, separate from fulfilment.
  4. Over-receipt isn't capped. Receiving more than you ordered is allowed (12 against an order of 10 still reads Received). Left open for now.
