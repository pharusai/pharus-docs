platform.user_invitations

Invitations issued by an org to an email. Lives in platform.* because the "List my pending invitations" flow is cross-tenant by definition — by then a user can be invited to orgs they don't currently belong to. See Q2.

Columns​

ColumnTypeNotes
idUUID PK
org_idUUID FK→ platform.organizations(id) ON DELETE CASCADE.
invited_byUUID FK→ platform.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE. Identity, not membership.
emailVARCHARTarget email. Not necessarily a users.email yet.
roleVARCHARRole to grant when accepted.
tokenUUIDUNIQUE. Acceptance link.
expires_atTIMESTAMPTZDefault NOW() + 7 days.
accepted_atTIMESTAMPTZNULL until accepted. Mutually exclusive with revoked_at.
revoked_atTIMESTAMPTZNULL until revoked.
created_atTIMESTAMPTZDefault NOW().

Read patterns​

  • Per-org listing (GET /invitations) — WHERE org_id = ? AND accepted_at IS NULL AND revoked_at IS NULL. Powers the Settings → Users → pending invitations panel.
  • Cross-user listing (GET /me/invitations) — WHERE email = ? AND accepted_at IS NULL AND revoked_at IS NULL. Powers the "you've been invited to these orgs" UI.

Both are cheap with the platform-side placement; neither would survive cleanly if invitations lived in per-org schemas.

Why invited_by references identity, not membership​

If invited_by referenced organization_users(id), removing the inviter from the org (revoking their membership) would leave dangling FKs or force ON DELETE rules that erase the audit trail. Identity is stable; membership isn't.
